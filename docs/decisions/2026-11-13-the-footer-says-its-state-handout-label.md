# The footer says its state in a line, and red is only for danger (owner, 2026-11-13)

Tried six footer designs side by side and had Haiku read the 43 screens of InterstellarBG without help. What it misread most was not the wording but whether a button could be pressed or had been pressed: it could not tell the pressed next button from one still to press, it could not tell why two grey buttons were grey, and it could not tell that a greyed next button waits for the settings. So the upper row carries one sentence of state (`status.*`: waiting for others, the other's turn, fill the settings, who has to tap), and the buttons keep their labels, size and place. A pressed toggle (次へ) is outlined with a dark 2px edge and a tick instead of red (`--color-undo` is removed). Red is kept for danger (`--color-danger`, `Button variant="danger"`), and a danger button is never the rightmost of the main row, where the button to press now sits. Not taken: shorter labels with symbols (they do not say what happens), a second line inside the buttons (it makes the footer taller in landscape), a separate cancel button (it breaks the toggle decision). The upper row's read-back button reads ハンドアウト (`footer.handout`, was "HO"): the owner chose it over "HOを読み返す", which was recommended, to avoid the abbreviation. The sentence for a button anyone can tap for everyone is kept short (`status.anyoneNext`: 誰かが押すと進みます) so that it and the 6-character button name share one row on a 390px phone.
