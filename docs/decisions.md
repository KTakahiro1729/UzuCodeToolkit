# Decisions

Owner decisions for this repository, newest last.

## 2026-11-11

- **Do not show who reads next.** In the two-player sample the lines alternate, so the screen shows only the lines read so far and the current one. When the newest line is yours, it is your turn.
- **Font: Shippori Mincho.** Bundled as a woff2 subset of the story text's characters (`samples/hundred-arms/src/fonts/`) and rebuilt when the script's characters change (steps in `CLAUDE.md`).
- **Chat-style screen with icons.** Lines appear as chat bubbles with the speaker's icon; your role's lines on the right, the other role's on the left. Icons are assumed to exist (`public/assets/icon-<role>.webp`, also used by the manifest's character select); the current files are placeholders: Noto Color Emoji glyphs (🗡️ for 勇, 🐙 for 百) rendered once to webp, so they look the same on every device.
- **Light theme, usual chat-app colours.** Soft grey page (not pure white); the current line is white, or a calm blue when it is yours; read lines sit on quieter bubbles with full text contrast. Tokens live in `samples/hundred-arms/src/theme.css`; screens use those variables, never raw colours. Every text colour meets WCAG AA (4.5:1); no opacity fades on resting states.
- **Post-game screen shows the afterword.** After the last line, the title and end mark hold for a moment, then they fade out and the post-game screen fades in after them (200 ms each, one at a time, instant with reduced motion): the centred greeting `review.heading` and under it an area labelled `review.afterword` holding the author's afterword (`afterword` in `script.json`, the author's words verbatim, CC BY 4.0 with the script). No tap needed.
- **Design covers function only.** Design here means helping players see what to do and keeping cognitive load low; decoration (hues, typeface, icon art) is out of scope. Screens are checked against six things to avoid: excess motion (stutter counts), unclear state or action, no clear focus, the eye drawn elsewhere, reading the obvious, and eye travel. Details in `docs/design.md`.
- **Show what to do on your turn.** Right under the newest line, both players see the advance button: filled for the reader, greyed for the other. It says `hint.readThenTap` until your first line is read, then shows the next mark alone; on the last line it says `button.end` instead. Tapping elsewhere does nothing. Each line shows the speaker's name; in the top band your role's icon has a double circle, and band text is bold.
- **Fonts by role.** Story text in Shippori Mincho, UI text in BIZ UDPGothic (Google Fonts, bundled as subsets like the mincho), as two CSS variables. No text below 16px.
- **The line being read matches the button.** When you read, your current line takes the advance button's blue (light, saturated, dark text). The other role's lines are always white.
- **The band shares the HUD's row only when the HUD is narrow.** Whether the top band gets its own row under the UZU HUD is decided by the HUD's share of the screen width: over a quarter (upright phones) it gets its own row; a quarter or less (landscape, tablets) it sits beside the HUD on the same row.
- **Any number of players, and one player reading several roles.** The engine takes any player count; UZU documents no upper limit (`playerCount` is 1 or more), and its catalogue lists works for up to five. Each seat plays a part (a manifest character) that can hold several roles, as in `samples/mittsu-no-takara`. Details in `CLAUDE.md`, Players and parts.
- **Stage directions have no background.** They read as narration, as plain text across the chat column; the advance button alone shows whose turn it is.
- **Ruby above the word, at half size.** Readings from the script show as ruby above their word, the one exception to the 16px minimum, rather than inline in brackets at full size. Ruby may run past its word instead of spreading the word's letters.
- **A stage direction you read gets side lines.** While you read a stage direction, a line in the advance button's colour stands at each side of it, instead of colouring the whole block.
- **Playing cards keep their court figures.** The deck is Adrian Kennard's CC0 SVG cards with the figure art on J, Q and K (about 310 KB for 54 cards), not a plain version; each face loads the first time it shows.
